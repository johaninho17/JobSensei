# Application Rating Rubric

Produce a calibrated `Fit Score` and a separate `Apply Recommendation`. The Fit Score measures demonstrated evidence; the recommendation decides whether a credible transferable path makes the role worth applying to. A positive recommendation never increases the Fit Score.

| Category | Weight | What to inspect |
|---|---:|---|
| Core responsibilities | 40% | Ability to perform the central work described in the JD |
| Technical and functional capabilities | 25% | Required skills, methods, systems, and analytical or technical foundations |
| Scope, communication, and seniority | 15% | Customer or stakeholder scope, ownership level, collaboration, and role level |
| Relevant outcomes | 10% | Supported results, problem types, and comparable execution |
| Domain and tool familiarity | 5% | Industry, platform, and employer-specific experience, including adjacent experience |
| Learning and transferability | 5% | Ability to carry adjacent experience into the role and learn missing specifics |

Classify every material JD requirement before scoring. A title, base-resume wording, generic role label, or the JD itself cannot make a requirement direct.

- `direct`: 1.0, full credit
- `strong_transferable`: maximum 0.80, substantial but incomplete credit
- `adjacent_transferable`: maximum 0.65, useful foundation credit
- `unclear`: maximum 0.25, low credit and reduced confidence
- `gap`: 0, no credit

Weight requirements by importance and whether they are reasonably required on entry. Group preferred tools, employer systems, and learnable domain knowledge instead of scoring each as a separate gap. Demonstrated learning supports ramp readiness and the Apply Recommendation but never erases a role-defining gap or turns transferable work into direct experience.

Use exactly six capability rows, one per category above. Group related requirements within the appropriate row. Fold soft adjectives such as rigorous, detail-oriented, collaborative, proactive, or excellent communicator into observable QA, validation, stakeholder, or delivery work instead of scoring them separately. Keep logistics outside weighted capability fit unless they are genuine hard gates.

Report category scores, weighted arithmetic, confidence, direct strengths, transferable strengths, gaps, contradictions, the Apply Recommendation, and two or three reasons the score is not higher. A score does not represent hiring probability.

Use these interpretation bands:

- `4.5–5.0`: exceptional direct fit. Require at least 80% of weighted core responsibilities to be direct, with no material core gap.
- `4.0–4.4`: strong fit. Permit no more than one material transferable core requirement.
- `3.5–3.9`: credible, apply-worthy fit. Transferability can justify applying but does not make the candidate near-direct.
- `3.0–3.4`: borderline; show the user the tradeoffs
- `<3.0`: weak fit or meaningful blocker

Use `high` confidence only when at least 75% of weighted material requirements are direct, with no major gap or source conflict. Use `medium` for mixed direct/transferable evidence and `low` for unclear or primarily transferable core evidence.

Hard blockers are reported separately. Only genuine non-negotiable constraints, such as required authorization, mandatory location, licensing, or an essential qualification that cannot reasonably be learned, can force `manual_review` or `screened_out` regardless of the weighted score. Missing preferred tools or exact domain experience must reduce fit or confidence and create a risk note, not automatically fail the job.
