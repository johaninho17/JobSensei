# Interview Memory Contract

Store reusable interview learning under `data/debrief/interview-memory/`, outside individual job workspaces.

## Records

- `role-index.json` contains normalized role families, tags, source job IDs, artifact paths, and update timestamps.
- `<role-family>.md` contains generalized question patterns, recurring feedback, story categories that worked, weak-answer patterns, and coaching adjustments.
- Do not copy complete transcripts, employer-specific facts, candidate reports, salary details, or confidential information into generalized memory.

## Similarity

Normalize title case, punctuation, common seniority terms, and aliases such as `FDE` to `forward deployed engineer`. Reuse memory only when:

- the normalized role family matches; or
- at least two meaningful role tokens overlap and the responsibilities support the same family.

Treat adjacent customer-facing engineering roles, such as forward deployed, solutions, and sales engineering, as related only when their JD responsibilities overlap. Record the reason for every memory match.

## Read Boundary

Read the role index first, then only the matched role-memory file. Do not scan prior job folders or open old mock transcripts during normal preparation. A user-selected prior job may be read directly under the active-context rules.

Memory is coaching context, not career evidence. It cannot prove that the candidate performed work, that a company asks a question, or that a current interview follows a prior process.

## Write Boundary

Update memory only after a mock, feedback session, or `debrief_analysis.md` has been completed and saved. Generalize the lesson, retain a pointer to its source artifact, and preserve earlier useful entries. Initial interview preparation does not create a success claim.

Record the stage that produced each lesson. Recruiter-screen patterns should not dominate technical-round preparation, and technical-loop patterns should not displace first-call questions. A debrief may improve question distribution and coaching but never proves that the candidate has a skill or that another employer follows the same process.
